<Section icon={Map} title="How BRASS HELL Upgrade Costs Work">

Upgrading is the backbone of progression in BRASS HELL, but the cost curve punishes players who spend without a plan. Every upgrade tier costs more than the last, and late tiers demand currencies that are slow to farm. The result is simple: a player who spreads resources across five half-finished upgrades will always fall behind a player who finishes two lines completely.

Understanding three principles keeps your account healthy:

- **Escalating costs**: each tier typically costs noticeably more than the previous one, while the stat gain per tier stays flat or shrinks, so early tiers offer the best value per resource spent.
- **Currencies differ in scarcity**: common materials gate early upgrades, while rare drops and boss-limited currencies gate the top tiers. Spend rare currency only after your build direction is locked in.
- **Diminishing returns**: once an upgrade line stops meaningfully changing your clear speed or survivability, redirect resources elsewhere.

<Callout type="tip" title="Cost-to-Power Rule">
Before buying any upgrade, ask one question: does this shorten my next ten runs or keep me alive in content I am actually attempting? If the answer is no, hold your currency.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step Upgrade Planning">

Follow this sequence each time you sit down to spend resources. It takes two minutes and prevents the most expensive mistakes in BRASS HELL.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Audit Your Resources">
    List what currencies and materials you currently hold, and note which ones are capped or time-gated. Time-gated currency should always be spent before it overflows.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Identify Your Bottleneck">
    Determine whether you are dying, clearing too slowly, or unable to afford the next tier. Each bottleneck points to a different upgrade priority.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Compare Cost-to-Power">
    Check the cost of the next tier for each candidate upgrade and estimate the impact. Favor cheap tiers with meaningful gains over expensive tiers with marginal ones.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Commit and Finish Lines">
    Spend into your chosen line until it hits a natural breakpoint, then stop. Finishing one line beats partially starting three.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Re-evaluate After Each Milestone">
    After clearing a new stage or difficulty, repeat the audit. Bottlenecks shift as content gets harder, and yesterday's priority may no longer apply.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="Checkpoint Habit">
Review your upgrade plan once per session, not mid-run. Decisions made between runs are rational; decisions made mid-run are impulsive.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Where to Farm Upgrade Materials">

Upgrade costs are only half the equation — the other half is income. Match your farming location to the material you need rather than grinding a single stage on repeat.

| Material Type | Best Source Style | Efficiency Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common materials | Repeatable standard stages | Fast, unlimited, low value per run |
| Rare drops | Boss and elite encounters | Limited attempts, high value |
| Time-gated currency | Daily and weekly content | Always spend before cap overflow |
| Event materials | Limited-time event modes | Prioritize while the event is live |

Key farming principles:

- **Run the hardest stage you can clear consistently**, not the hardest stage you can clear occasionally. Wipes waste time and yield nothing.
- **Never let capped currency sit at maximum**. Overflowed income is effectively lost income.
- **Event modes usually beat permanent farming** for upgrade materials while they are active, so shift your schedule around them.

<Callout type="tip" title="Efficiency Multiplier">
Any upgrade that increases drop rate or clear speed effectively reduces every future upgrade cost. Buy these early and the rest of the game gets cheaper.
</Callout>
