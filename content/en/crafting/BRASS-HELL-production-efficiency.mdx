<Section icon={Factory} title="Understanding BRASS HELL Production Efficiency">

Production efficiency in BRASS HELL measures how effectively your factory converts raw ore into finished brass components per cycle. Every machine in your production chain consumes power, space, and time, and any weak link drags down total output. A well-run facility keeps every machine busy, minimizes transit distance, and matches supply rates to demand.

The three pillars of efficiency are:

- **Uptime** — machines should never sit idle waiting for inputs
- **Balance** — upstream stages should slightly outproduce downstream stages
- **Density** — shorter conveyor runs mean fewer delays and fewer resource losses

| Metric | What It Measures | Target Value |
|---|---|---|
| Machine Uptime | % of cycle time actively working | **90%+** |
| Input Buffer | Raw stock held at each stage | 1-2 cycles of demand |
| Transit Ratio | Conveyor length vs. machine count | Under 6 tiles per machine |
| Power Surplus | Unused grid capacity | 10-15% headroom |
| Defect Rate | Scrapped output per batch | Below 2% |

<Callout type="warning" title="Watch Your First Bottleneck">
Most efficiency losses in BRASS HELL trace back to a single underfed machine. Audit your chain from ore input to final assembly before adding new production lines — expanding a bottlenecked factory only wastes resources.
</Callout>

When comparing layouts, weigh expansion cost against current output needs. The compact cell wins on flexibility, while the layered stack wins on ceiling — but only if your power grid supports it.

| Layout | Space Needed | Power Load | Expansion Ease | Output Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Compact Cell | Low | Low | ★★★★★ | Medium |

<Callout type="tip" title="Spacing Rule">
Leave one tile of walkable space between production cells. It costs almost nothing in efficiency but makes repairs, upgrades, and rerouting far faster during expansion phases.

<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step Efficiency Overhaul">

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Audit Your Current Chain">
    Trace your production from ore intake to finished goods. Note every machine that sits idle or runs backed-up. Idle machines signal oversupply downstream; backed-up buffers signal a bottleneck ahead.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Identify the Bottleneck Stage">
    The stage with the longest queue of unprocessed inputs is your true output limit. No upgrade anywhere else will increase final output until this stage is expanded or sped up.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Rebalance Machine Ratios">
    Add machines to the bottleneck stage until its queue stabilizes, then re-check the next stage downstream. Repeat until every queue moves steadily.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Upgrade Power and Transit">
    With ratios balanced, confirm your grid has 10-15% headroom and shorten the longest conveyor runs. Power brownouts silently throttle machine speed.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Buffer and Monitor">
    Place small input buffers at each stage so brief interruptions upstream don't cascade. Recheck your metrics table after every expansion.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="Verify the Fix">
After rebalancing, your final assembly machines should run near-continuous output. If finished goods still arrive in bursts, a hidden bottleneck remains — repeat the audit from Step 1.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Common Mistakes That Kill Efficiency">

- **Expanding before auditing** — new lines fed by a bottlenecked supply chain just add idle machines
- **Over-prioritizing refineries** — refineries are power-hungry; brass smelting and core assembly should draw power first
- **Single long conveyor lines** — long runs add transit delay and single points of failure
- **Ignoring power headroom** — running the grid at 100% invites brownouts that throttle every machine
- **Skipping buffers** — without input buffers, one hiccup stalls the entire chain

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Unbalanced ratios | Queues pile at one stage | Add machines to bottleneck |
| Power shortage | Random slow cycles | Build 10-15% grid headroom |
| Long transit runs | Bursty output | Compact layout or buffers |
| No buffers | Chain-wide stalls | Small input stock per stage |
| Overbuilt refineries | Power drain, low gain | Prioritize smelting and assembly |

<Callout type="warning" title="Don't Chase Raw Speed">
Faster machines fed by an under-supplied line produce nothing. Balance supply first, then invest in speed upgrades where queues are consistently full.
</Callout>

</Section>
