<Section icon={Wrench} title="How Bullet Crafting Works">

Bullet crafting in BRASS HELL is the heart of the factory loop. Every round follows the same physical sequence: press the machine, drop a shell into the tray, swipe to the powder, drag the correct amount in, then seat the projectile. Early on you make one bullet per cycle, which feels slow — that is by design. The entire progression curve is about converting that tedious manual loop into an automated production line that spits out sealed boxes worth millions.

<Section icon={Map} title="Caliber Progression Order">

Calibers unlock in sequence, and each one raises both the cost of materials and the payout per box. Jumping to a new caliber the moment it unlocks is almost always correct, because older rounds become trivially low value by comparison.

| Caliber | Rounds per Cycle | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| .22 LR | 1 → 2 | Starter round, good for hunting demand, tiny margins |
| 9 mm | 3 | Popular caliber, solid mid-early income |
| .45 ACP | 5 | Fun round, noticeably better batch value |
| .357 | ~10+ | Big batch era begins, ~$890 per delivery |
| 5.56 | 25 | Major value jump, boxes sell for ~$1,000 |
| 7.62 | ~50 | Late-game staple, thousands per batch |
| .308 | 50+ | Bridging caliber before heavy rounds |
| .50 BMG | 100 | Gigantic brass, ~$500,000 per delivery |
| 20 mm | 100 | Final rifle-class round, up to $16 million per batch |
| 30 mm | 100 | Aircraft/anti-air class, top-tier rifle production |
| Shotgun shells | — | Separate line from 10 gauge up; requires machine restart |

<Callout type="warning" title="Machine Reset Cost">
When you finish 30 mm and move to shotgun shells, management resets your machine — most parts do not transfer to shell production. Treat 30 mm as the natural endpoint of your rifle build.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Swords} title="Step-by-Step: Your First 1,000 Bullets">

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Master the Manual Loop">
    Craft .22 rounds one at a time until the drop-swipe-drag-press rhythm is muscle memory. Sell every box immediately to fund your first upgrades.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Buy Rounds-Per-Cycle First">
    Upgrade to two rounds per cycle, then three trips per cycle. Batch what you can — even small bulk runs beat single deliveries.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Invest in Powder Early">
    Grab every powder tier as soon as it is affordable. The 1.5x jump is immediately noticeable in your payout per delivery.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Climb Calibers Aggressively">
    Move to 9 mm, .45 ACP, then .357 the moment each unlocks. Old calibers stop being worth your press time quickly.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Automate and Scale">
    Purchase auto powder, auto press, the packing bench, and a helper. Upgrade storage to 1,500 materials and seal massive batches of 7.62 and beyond.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Section icon={Shield} title="The 4:17 Loop & Money Habits">

BRASS HELL hides a strange narrative under the factory grind: every radio log, receipt, and handover record is stamped **4:17 a.m.**, and no relief crew ever signs in. Story beats push you between the powder stores and dispatch, but the work never stops — and neither do the payouts. Embrace it: even if you are stuck at 4:17 forever, you may as well be making money.

There is also a shooting range where you can use your own ammunition to earn money, but be aware that feeding guns burns through rounds you could otherwise sell — two full magazines can eat a heavy batch fast.

<Callout type="warning" title="Ammo Is Money">
Every round you fire at the range is a round you did not sell. Test the guns for fun, but keep the bulk of your production sealed for delivery.
</Callout>
