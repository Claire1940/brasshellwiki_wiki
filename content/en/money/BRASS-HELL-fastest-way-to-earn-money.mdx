<Section icon={Coins} title="How Money-Making Works in BRASS HELL">

BRASS HELL pays you for completed shipments, not for half-finished work. Every dollar you earn flows through one loop: gather materials, operate the ammunition press, craft finished rounds, pack them into crates, and dispatch the shipment. The fastest way to earn money is not a secret location or a hidden bonus — it is tightening that loop until your factory almost never sits idle.

Because the game runs on an incremental design, small efficiency gains compound. Each cycle you shave seconds off converts directly into more shipments per session, which funds upgrades, which speeds up future cycles. Players who treat dispatch as the *start* of the next order rather than the end of the previous one consistently out-earn players who pause after each payout.

<Section icon={Map} title="The Money Loop: Step-by-Step Production Cycle">

This is the exact cycle efficient players run inside the snowbound factory. Follow it in order and repeat until it becomes muscle memory.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Review the Active Order">
    Check exactly what the current order requires before touching anything. Knowing the target quantity and product prevents mid-cycle surprises and wasted trips.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Stage Materials Near the Press">
    Move every required input within arm's reach of the press. Use a fixed, repeatable route between the supply area and the machine to eliminate wandering.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Run the Press Without Gaps">
    Load materials, run the production interaction, collect finished rounds, and immediately reload. The press should only be idle when you physically cannot feed it.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Complete the Full Batch Before Moving">
    Finish the entire order quantity first, then carry everything to the crate area in one trip. Split deliveries double your walking time for no extra pay.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Pack, Dispatch, and Restart Instantly">
    Load the crate, finalize the shipment, dispatch it, and begin staging materials for the next order before the payout animation finishes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="Biggest Earning Killer">
The most common mistake is resting after a dispatch. Idle time between shipments is the single largest source of lost income in BRASS HELL — treat every second the press sits empty as unpaid time.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Package} title="Caliber Choice and Shipment Value">

Ammunition progression follows your factory's development, and the best caliber is simply the highest-value round you can produce *consistently*. A premium round that stalls your production line earns less than a cheaper round flowing through an uninterrupted cycle.

| Progression Stage | Round Type | Value Level | Best Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starting production | Starter round | Entry-level | Learn the loop, fund first upgrades |
| Early progression | Intermediate round | Improved | Adopt once basic production is consistent |
| Advanced progression | Advanced round | Highest tier | Main option when speed and materials support it |

Switch calibers only when your material flow and press speed can keep the full shipment cycle running without pauses. If upgrading your round introduces stalls, you are trading throughput for a label — step back down until your workshop catches up.

<Callout type="info" title="Consistency Beats Peak Value">
A steady stream of mid-tier shipments usually out-earn sporadic high-tier ones, because dispatch frequency — not single-order value — drives income in an incremental factory.
</Callout>
